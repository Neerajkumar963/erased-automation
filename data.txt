[Smedley Butler](orange) retired from the US Marines in 1931 as the most decorated marine in US history — twice awarded the [Medal of Honor](orange). headline
[JAPAN](orange) RAN [BIOLOGICAL WARFARE](red) EXPERIMENTS ON LIVING PEOPLE. THE [US](orange) GAVE THE SCIENTISTS IMMUNITY FOR THE DATA.

content
[Unit 731](orange) was a secret [Imperial Japanese Army](orange) biological and chemical warfare research unit that conducted lethal experiments on prisoners in occupied [Manchuria](orange) from 1935 to 1945. Experiments included live vivisection, freezing experiments, pressure chamber tests, and deliberate infection with plague and cholera. An estimated **3,000 people** were killed. After WW2, [US](orange) officials secretly granted immunity to Unit 731 scientists in exchange for their research data. The **Nuremberg prosecution** of German war criminals proceeded, but no Unit 731 scientist was prosecuted by the [US](orange).

headline
CHEVRON WAS ORDERED TO PAY $9.5 BILLION FOR AMAZON CONTAMINATION. IT REFUSED. THEN USED US COURTS TO BLOCK IT.

content
From 1964 to 1992, Texaco (later acquired by Chevron) extracted oil in Ecuador's Amazon, leaving behind oil pits, contaminated water sources, and health impacts on indigenous communities. An Ecuadorian court in 2011 ordered Chevron to pay $9.5 billion — later upheld by Ecuador's Supreme Court. Chevron refused to pay and withdrew all assets from Ecuador. It then pursued New York federal courts and obtained a ruling blocking enforcement of the Ecuadorian judgment anywhere in the world, based on claims the judgment was obtained through fraud. The $9.5 billion has never been paid.

headline
ROCKEFELLER'S COAL COMPANY CALLED THE NATIONAL GUARD ON STRIKING MINERS. 11 CHILDREN WERE KILLED.

content
In September 1913, miners at a Colorado Fuel and Iron Company mine — owned by John D. Rockefeller Jr. — went on strike for safer conditions. They were evicted from company housing and set up a tent colony at Ludlow. On April 20, 1914, the Colorado National Guard attacked the colony with rifles and machine guns. 21 people were killed: 2 women and 11 children were found suffocated in a pit beneath a burned tent. Rockefeller testified before Congress that he had not been aware of conditions. The subsequent national outrage led directly to federal labour reform discussions.

headline
MI5 OFFICERS BELIEVED THEIR OWN PRIME MINISTER WAS A SOVIET SPY. SOME PLOTTED TO REMOVE HIM FROM POWER.

content
In the 1970s, a faction within MI5 believed Prime Minister Harold Wilson was a Soviet intelligence asset. Senior MI5 officer Peter Wright — later the author of Spycatcher — claimed a group of officers mounted destabilisation campaigns against Wilson's government, including leaking damaging material to journalists and foreign governments. Wilson himself resigned unexpectedly in 1976 and later privately told BBC journalists he believed MI5 had been working against him. A 1988 Cabinet Office inquiry acknowledged some officers had exceeded their authority but full details have never been officially released.

headline
PAYDAY LOAN COMPANIES CHARGE 400% ANNUAL INTEREST. LEGALLY. IN 30 US STATES. THEY LOBBIED TO KEEP IT THAT WAY.

content
Payday loan companies offer short-term loans with fees that translate to Annual Percentage Rates of 300-400%. A two-week loan of $300 with a $45 fee = 391% APR. Studies show 80% of payday loans are rolled over or renewed within 14 days, trapping borrowers in debt cycles. The industry disproportionately targets low-income communities and communities of colour. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau attempted federal rate caps. The industry spent $13 million lobbying in 2019-2020 against regulation. 30 states still have no payday loan rate caps.

headline
THE GOVERNMENT PROSECUTED, IMPRISONED, AND DEPORTED THE LEADERS OF AMERICA'S LARGEST UNION UNTIL IT COLLAPSED.

content
The Industrial Workers of the World (IWW) — the "Wobblies" — organised hundreds of thousands of workers across industries by 1917. During WW1, the US government launched a systematic crackdown: using the Espionage Act of 1917 to prosecute IWW leadership for anti-war speech. In September 1917, federal agents raided 48 IWW offices simultaneously across the country and arrested 165 leaders. In the 1919-1921 Red Scare, foreign-born IWW members were deported. The IWW was effectively destroyed as a mass organisation. The Espionage Act charges from that era are still on the books.

headline
BRITAIN'S SECRETARY OF STATE FOR WAR WAS SHARING A MISTRESS WITH A SOVIET NAVAL OFFICER.

content
In 1963, John Profumo — UK Secretary of State for War, with access to NATO nuclear defence briefings — was revealed to have been involved with Christine Keeler, a young woman who was simultaneously involved with Captain Yevgeny Ivanov, a Soviet Naval Intelligence attaché. The extent of any information passed between the relationships was never fully established in court. Profumo lied to the House of Commons about his involvement, later admitted it, and resigned. The scandal severely damaged the Macmillan government and contributed to the Conservative Party's election loss in 1964.

content
McKesson, AmerisourceBergen, and Cardinal Health are the three largest pharmaceutical distributors in the US. Between 2006 and 2016, they shipped 76 billion opioid pills to West Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee, Virginia, Ohio, Alabama, Arkansas, Utah, and Nevada. Under DEA regulations, distributors are legally required to report and refuse suspicious orders — orders that appear to exceed what any pharmacy could legitimately dispense. The distributors repeatedly failed to do so. In 2022, all three agreed to pay a combined $21 billion to settle state and local lawsuits.

headline
AN 8-STOREY FACTORY COLLAPSED IN 2013. 1,132 WORKERS DIED MAKING WESTERN FASHION. CRACKS WERE REPORTED THE DAY BEFORE.

content
On April 23, 2013, structural cracks were discovered in the Rana Plaza building in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Banks and shops on lower floors closed for safety reasons. Garment workers on upper floors were told to return to work anyway — suppliers reportedly faced penalties for missed delivery deadlines. On April 24th, the 8-storey building collapsed. 1,132 people were killed. At least 2,500 were injured. The building housed factories supplying clothes to Primark, Mango, Walmart, and other Western retailers. The Bangladesh Accord on Fire and Building Safety was created in direct response.

headline
THE CIA AND SOVIET KGB WERE SIMULTANEOUSLY FUNDING OPPOSITE SIDES OF THE SAME CIVIL WAR IN AFRICA.

content
When Angola became independent from Portugal in November 1975, three competing factions immediately fought for control. The CIA covertly funded and armed the FNLA and UNITA factions under Operation FEATURE. The Soviet Union funded the MPLA and arranged for Cuban troops to deploy. Congress passed the Clark Amendment in 1975 specifically prohibiting US military involvement in Angola after the operation became known. Declassified documents show the CIA had deployed $32 million and the KGB had deployed significant support — both without the knowledge of most of their own governments' citizens.

content
From 1994 to 2009, the NFL ran its own Mild Traumatic Brain Injury Committee, which repeatedly published research denying a link between football and long-term brain damage, despite neuropathologist Dr. Bennet Omalu publishing peer-reviewed research in 2005 linking football to Chronic Traumatic Encephalopathy (CTE). Internal House of Representatives correspondence revealed in 2009 showed NFL officials were aware of CTE risks while publicly disputing them. The NFL settled a class-action lawsuit with 20,000 former players for $1 billion in 2015, without admitting liability.

headline
KILL THE INDIAN, SAVE THE MAN" — THE US GOVERNMENT'S OFFICIAL APPROACH TO NATIVE AMERICAN CHILDREN.

content
Carlisle Indian Industrial School in Pennsylvania was founded in 1879 by Captain Richard Henry Pratt, who coined the phrase: "Kill the Indian in him, and save the man." The school — funded by the US federal government — was the model for a nationwide network of 408 boarding schools that forcibly separated Native American children from their families, banned Indigenous languages and ceremonies, and forced assimilation through English education and Christian religion. A 2022 US Interior Department report documented 53 burial sites near these schools and confirmed children died in the schools and were buried far from their families.

headline
THE WHITE HOUSE SOLD ARMS TO IRAN UNDER AN EMBARGO. FUNNELED THE MONEY TO REBEL FIGHTERS. THEN SHREDDED THE DOCUMENTS.

content
In 1986, it was revealed that senior White House officials — including National Security Council aide Oliver North — had secretly sold weapons to Iran, which was under a US arms embargo, and diverted the profits to fund Contra rebels in Nicaragua — a funding mechanism Congress had explicitly prohibited. When investigators arrived, North and his secretary shredded thousands of documents. North was convicted on three counts in 1989 but the convictions were vacated on appeal due to immunity deals. President Reagan said he had no knowledge of the diversion. 11 officials were convicted, and 6 were later pardoned by President George H.W. Bush.

content
Navient — spun off from Sallie Mae in 2014 — serviced $300 billion in federal and private student loans for 12 million borrowers. In 2017, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau sued Navient, alleging it systematically steered borrowers away from income-driven repayment programmes — which would have lowered monthly payments — and into forbearance plans that cost more long-term, because the processing was simpler for the company. In 2022, Navient reached a $1.85 billion settlement with 39 state attorneys general and cancelled $1.7 billion in private student loans. Navient did not admit wrongdoing.

headline
US SOLDIERS WERE ORDERED TO WATCH NUCLEAR BOMB TESTS FROM TRENCHES. THEN TOLD NEVER TO SPEAK OF IT.

content
Between 1945 and 1963, approximately 400,000 US military personnel were present at nuclear tests in Nevada, the Pacific, and elsewhere — often positioned in trenches a few miles from detonation sites to test how infantry could operate after nuclear exchange. Many were ordered to march into the blast area immediately after detonation. They were told radiation exposure was minimal. Veterans later reported unusually high rates of cancer. Many were told under the Atomic Energy Act they could face prosecution for discussing their exposure. Congress only formally recognised these veterans and created a compensation programme in 1988.

headline
IN 1983, NATO WAR GAMES WERE SO REALISTIC THAT SOVIET FORCES WENT TO NUCLEAR ALERT. NOBODY IN THE WEST KNEW.

content
In November 1983, NATO conducted Able Archer 83 — a military exercise simulating escalation to nuclear warfare. Soviet intelligence interpreted the exercises as a possible cover for a real US first strike. Soviet nuclear forces in Eastern Europe were placed on heightened alert. Soviet aircraft armed with nuclear weapons were prepared for immediate launch. The crisis passed when the exercises ended. President Reagan only learned how close the situation came in 1984 when a CIA report was declassified. A 2015 UK government report concluded the world had come "closer to nuclear war than was realised at the time."

headline
DRUG COMPANIES EXTEND THEIR PATENT MONOPOLIES FOR DECADES BY MAKING MINOR CHANGES. ONE INSULIN WAS MODIFIED 74 TIMES.

content
Under US patent law, pharmaceutical companies can reset their patent clock by making minor modifications to existing drugs — a practice known as "evergreening." Modifications can include: a new delivery mechanism, a slightly different molecular form, or a new dosage. I-MAK analysis found one insulin product underwent 74 reformulations over its patent lifetime. The net effect: generic competitors are blocked from entering the market for decades beyond the original 20-year patent term. The Federal Trade Commission has identified evergreening as a primary driver of drug price inflation in the US. Over 70% of the drugs most commonly prescribed were reformulations of existing products.

headline
A "COMPLETELY SAFE" MORNING SICKNESS DRUG CAUSED BIRTH DEFECTS IN OVER 10,000 CHILDREN WORLDWIDE.

content
In the late 1950s, the drug Thalidomide was marketed across Europe, Canada, Australia, and dozens of other countries as a safe treatment for morning sickness and insomnia in pregnant women. The manufacturer, Grünenthal, advertised it as "completely safe." It caused severe limb and organ malformations in an estimated 10,000 to 20,000 children worldwide, with thousands more miscarried. In the US, FDA medical officer Dr. Frances Kelsey repeatedly refused to approve the drug, citing insufficient safety data — a decision that spared the US from the scale of the crisis seen elsewhere. The disaster directly led to the 1962 Kefauver-Harris Amendment, which created modern US drug safety and efficacy testing requirements.
