Caption (paste ready)
On February 14, 1884, a 25-year-old Theodore Roosevelt was called home from the state assembly.

His mother, Mittie, died of typhoid fever in the morning. Just hours later, in the very same house, his young wife Alice died from Bright's disease, two days after giving birth to their first child.

Devastated, Roosevelt drew a large black 'X' in his diary for that day and wrote a single sentence: 'The light has gone out of my life.'

Source: Theodore Roosevelt diary entries 1884, Library of Congress.

Victor Hugo — Driven Mad by Grief
1802–1885
Photo
19th century portrait of an older, serious Victor Hugo with a beard
HIS ELDEST DAUGHTER DROWNED AT 19. HER HUSBAND DROWNED TRYING TO SAVE HER.
Read the caption
Photo Sourcing
✅ Hugo's historical photos are public domain. Search Wikimedia Commons.
Caption (paste ready)
In 1843, Victor Hugo's beloved eldest daughter Léopoldine was boating on the Seine river when the vessel overturned.

Weighed down by her heavy Victorian skirts, the 19-year-old was pulled under. Her young husband, Charles Vacquerie, dove in to save her, but realizing he couldn't, he chose to drown alongside her.

Hugo, traveling at the time, found out about their deaths by reading a newspaper in a café. The grief permanently altered his life, leading to decades of dark, tragic poetry.

Source: Victor Hugo biographical archives, Les Contemplations.

Charles Darwin was a devoted father, but his life was shattered in 1851 when his favorite child, 10-year-old Annie, fell ill with suspected tuberculosis.

Darwin watched helplessly as she suffered for weeks before dying. The tragedy fundamentally broke him.

Already wrestling with the implications of his theory of evolution, Annie's agonizing death stripped away his remaining belief in a benevolent God. He stopped attending church entirely, waiting outside on Sundays while his family went in.

✅ Mary Todd Lincoln's photographs are public domain.
Caption (paste ready)
Mary Todd Lincoln's life was defined by unrelenting loss. She buried three of her four sons before they reached adulthood: Eddie at age 3, Willie at age 11, and Tad at age 18.

In 1865, she was sitting beside her husband, Abraham Lincoln, holding his hand when he was assassinated at Ford's Theatre.

Decades later, her erratic behavior and deep depression led her only surviving son, Robert, to have her involuntarily committed to a psychiatric asylum in 1875. She never forgave him.

Albert Einstein's youngest son, Eduard, was a gifted medical student who dreamed of becoming a psychiatrist.

But in 1930, at age 20, Eduard suffered a severe mental breakdown and was diagnosed with schizophrenia. He was institutionalized at the Burghölzli psychiatric hospital in Zurich.

When Einstein fled the Nazis for America in 1933, he left his son behind in Switzerland. Einstein never saw Eduard again, despite living for another 22 years. Eduard spent the rest of his life in the asylum, dying at age 55.

Source: Einstein family correspondence, biographical archives.

Marie Curie — Death by Carriage
1867–1934
Photo
Early 1900s portrait of Marie and Pierre Curie
HER HUSBAND WAS CRUSHED TO DEATH IN THE STREET. SHE KEPT HIS BLOOD-STAINED CLOTHES FOR THE REST OF HER LIFE.
Read the caption
Photo Sourcing
✅ Curie family historical photos are public domain.
Caption (paste ready)
Marie and Pierre Curie were inseparable partners in life and science, jointly winning a Nobel Prize. But their partnership ended violently in 1906.

Walking in the heavy rain in Paris, Pierre slipped and fell under a horse-drawn carriage. The heavy wagon wheel crushed his skull, killing him instantly in the middle of the street.

Marie was plunged into a deep depression, refusing to speak of him. She famously took his blood-stained clothes from the accident and kept them hidden in a closet until her own death from radiation poisoning 28 years later.

Source: Madame Curie by Eve Curie, biographical archives.

1755–1804

✅ Hamilton's painted portraits are public domain.
Caption (paste ready)
In 1801, Alexander Hamilton's 19-year-old eldest son, Philip, challenged a lawyer to a duel for insulting his father in a speech.

Philip was shot and died in agony the next day, surrounded by his weeping parents. The tragedy broke Hamilton's daughter Angelica, who suffered a mental breakdown and never recovered, spending the rest of her life speaking to Philip as if he were still alive.

Exactly three years later, Alexander Hamilton himself was killed in a duel against Aaron Burr. He was shot in the exact same spot in New Jersey where his son had died.

Source: Hamilton biographical archives, historical records.

Caption (paste ready)
Thomas Jefferson is remembered as a Founding Father, but his private life was overshadowed by constant mourning.

He married Martha Wayles in 1772. Over ten years, she endured six difficult pregnancies, which destroyed her health. She died months after giving birth to their last child at just 33 years old. Jefferson fainted at her bedside and was incapacitated by grief for weeks.

Of their six children, four died in early childhood. By the time Jefferson died decades later, only one of his legitimate children, Martha, was still alive.

Source: Jefferson Monticello archives, family letters.

King George III had long suffered from bouts of mental illness, but he always managed to recover. That ended in 1810.

1571–1630
Photo

Read the caption
Photo Sourcing
✅ Kepler's painted portraits are public domain.
Caption (paste ready)
Johannes Kepler discovered the laws of planetary motion, but his life on Earth was a nightmare.

In 1611, three of his children contracted smallpox, and his six-year-old son died. Shortly after, his wife Barbara died of Hungarian spotted fever (typhus/cholera).

If that wasn't enough, his mother, Katharina, was accused of witchcraft in 1615. Kepler spent six years legally defending her as she was imprisoned and shown instruments of torture. She was finally acquitted but died months later from the trauma of her imprisonment.

Source: Kepler's biographical archives, historical witchcraft trial records.

J.R.R. Tolkien — Orphaned in Poverty
1892–1973
Photo
Early 20th century photograph of a young J.R.R. Tolkien
HIS FATHER DIED WHEN HE WAS 4. HIS MOTHER DIED DESTITUTE WHEN HE WAS 12. HE WAS RAISED BY A PRIEST.
Read the caption
Photo Sourcing
⚠️ Ensure you use a verified public domain or fair-use image of early Tolkien.
Caption (paste ready)
Before he created Middle-earth, J.R.R. Tolkien's own world was destroyed twice.

His father died of rheumatic fever in South Africa when Tolkien was just four. His mother, Mabel, moved them back to England, but was disowned by her family after converting to Catholicism, plunging them into severe poverty.

When Tolkien was 12, his mother collapsed into a diabetic coma and died, a death he always blamed on the stress of their destitution. Orphaned and traumatized, he and his brother were placed in the care of a local Catholic priest.

Source: J.R.R. Tolkien: A Biography by Humphrey Carpenter.
