Franklin D. Roosevelt is celebrated as the monumental President who guided America through the Great Depression and WWII, all while secretly confined to a wheelchair due to severe polio. His wife, Eleanor, is revered as a fiercely independent, highly influential First Lady. But their legendary political partnership was actually born from a deeply agonizing, devastating personal betrayal that completely destroyed their actual marriage.

In 1918, while FDR was recovering from severe pneumonia, Eleanor unpacked his suitcase and discovered a massive, hidden bundle of deeply passionate love letters. They were from Lucy Mercer, Eleanor's own trusted, personal social secretary. The discovery completely shattered Eleanor. She offered FDR an immediate divorce, which would have entirely destroyed his political career.

Under massive pressure from his wealthy, domineering mother, FDR agreed to permanently end the affair to save his career, and Eleanor agreed to stay married. But the profound trust was completely, irrevocably broken. Eleanor later admitted that the bottom had entirely dropped out of her personal world. From that day forward, they lived entirely separate private lives, maintaining a cold, purely political alliance.

When FDR was suddenly struck by agonizing, paralyzing polio three years later, Eleanor dutifully became his legs and his political surrogate, but the deep romantic love was dead. In a final, devastating, heartbreaking twist, when FDR died of a massive stroke in Georgia in 1945, Eleanor discovered that Lucy Mercer had been secretly in the room with him, proving he had broken his promise to her until his dying breath.
